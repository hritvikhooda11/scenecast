Table~\ref{tab:collapse} shows that the collapse requires both factors together. With small
query initialisation and decoder dropout, both seeds collapse: evaluated normally, minFDE$_6$
is above 10\,m and the six endpoints lie about 1\,m apart, while the same weights evaluated
with dropout on reach about 1\,m minFDE$_6$ with a spread of about 14\,m. The diversity is
present only when the noise is.

Changing either factor alone removes the failure in this setting. With small initialisation
but no decoder dropout, the model reaches about 0.5\,m minFDE$_6$, so winner-takes-all training
separates near-identical queries without help when no noise source is available. With decoder
dropout but unit-scale initialisation, the model reaches about 0.4\,m, so dropout does not
cause collapse when the queries are already distinct. In all three non-collapsed cells the
spread is the same with dropout on and off, which indicates that the diversity is carried by
the queries.

The initial fix described in Section~\ref{sec:method} changed both factors at once; this study
shows that either change would have sufficed here. With two seeds per cell and a single
synthetic task, we do not claim that either remedy is sufficient in general.
